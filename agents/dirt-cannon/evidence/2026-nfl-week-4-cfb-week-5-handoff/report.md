## Methodology

Version: three-numbers-eligible-v1, continuing the prior issue. Efficiency = mean offensive EPA minus mean defensive EPA allowed. Expected points added estimates how a play changes the offense’s expected scoring value given the game situation. Consistency = share of eligible modeled plays with EPA > 0 on offense minus the corresponding defensive share. Big-play balance = share of all eligible snaps with a completed pass gain of at least 20 yards or a rush gain of at least 10 yards, generated minus allowed. Success/explosive differences use percentage points: 50% minus 40% equals 10 percentage points, not 10%.

NFL eligibility follows nflfastR run/pass classification, including sacks and scrambles, excluding kneels, spikes, no-plays, conversions and special teams. Penalty plays retain the source run/pass result only when a valid scrimmage play remains; no_play rows are excluded. Overtime is included. No garbage-time filter or opponent adjustment is applied. Missing EPA is excluded from both EPA and success denominators, never zero-filled. NFL coverage this run: 7,789 eligible plays, zero missing EPA or yards.

College uses the single cfbfastR EPA source, with rush/pass_attempt/sack flags, penalty_no_play exclusion and the documented text/type exclusions in compute_college.py. These provisional classifications are unchanged from the prior issue and are not represented as fully audited. NFL and college raw EPA scales are not treated as interchangeable.

Season results pool plays; they are not averages of game averages. Ranks use minimum rank for ties. Percentiles = 100 × (N − rank)/(N − 1), a rank-based descriptive convention, not probability. All NFL teams have four games, so a last-four-team-games window equals the season-to-date window here. Prior NFL published rank changes are included in CSV; no prior college national ranks are invented.

Selection: all 32 NFL teams were calculated first. Editorial shortlist: SF/BAL dominance, ATL/ARI/GB contradictions and MIA weakness. The repository selector orders those six; its interest ordering is editorial, not a predictive power rating.
